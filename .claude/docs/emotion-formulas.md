# Emotion Formulas — Reference, Not Checklist

> **Read this as background knowledge, not as a checklist to execute.**
>
> A writer SHOULD know these the way a chef knows the maillard reaction —
> as understanding of how emotions work in fiction.
>
> A writer MUST NOT mechanically pick one formula per chapter and "fill it
> in". That produces uniform, AI-shaped chapters. The formulas describe
> what's already happening when fiction works, not what to do to make
> fiction work.
>
> When in doubt: write the events that need to happen in this chapter,
> let the emotion follow. Don't reverse-engineer.

---

## § 1 — How Emotions Are Engineered

Each emotion has trigger conditions, mechanism, and pitfalls. The
mechanism explains *why* the emotion lands when conditions are met.

### Excitement (兴奋 / Catharsis)

**Formula:** `Sustained suppression + explosive release`

**Mechanism:** The release lands proportionally to the suppression.
5 chapters of suppression → one paragraph of release feels enormous.

**On-page markers:**
- A specific phrase or object referenced earlier returns at the moment
  of release (but **referenced once, not repeatedly** — see house-style
  Rule 5)
- Short sentences during the release moment
- An onlooker reacts with shock (this is how the reader knows the
  magnitude)

**Pitfalls:**
- Releasing on the same chapter as setup → flat
- Multiple releases in one chapter → diluted
- The opponent must have *earned* the punishment, otherwise it reads
  as bullying

---

### Sadness (悲伤)

**Formula:** `Deep attachment + irreversible loss`

**Mechanism:** Sadness requires the reader to have invested. New
characters cannot anchor sadness — established ones must.

**On-page markers:**
- A small detail (a habit, an object) that means nothing to outsiders
  but means everything to the protagonist
- The protagonist does not cry. Or cries silently. (Loud grief reads
  as performance.)
- Weather echoes (rain, snow, dusk) but does not announce itself
- The chapter does NOT end on the death scene — end on the silence
  after

**Pitfalls:**
- Killing a character for shock value → reader resents the author
- Over-describing the death itself instead of the aftermath
- Naming the emotion ("他很悲伤") instead of showing it

---

### Hype / Defiance (燃)

**Formula:** `Overwhelming odds + unwavering will`

**Mechanism:** The reader's chest tightens when a character bets
everything on principle.

**On-page markers:**
- One line of refusal that becomes the chapter's defining quote
- The protagonist's body language stays still even when threatened
- Onlookers — even enemies — momentarily hesitate

**Pitfalls:**
- The cost of refusing must be real → otherwise it's posturing
- Too many defiance scenes in a row → the trope becomes invisible

---

### Mystery (悬疑)

**Formula:** `Partial truth + suggestion of a larger truth`

**Mechanism:** The reader's brain refuses to put the book down —
incomplete patterns demand resolution.

**On-page markers:**
- A small inconsistency observed but not explained
- A character behaves out of character, briefly, with no comment from
  the narrator
- An object appears that shouldn't be there
- A date, a number, a name that doesn't match what we were told

**Pitfalls:**
- Withholding for the sake of withholding (no real answer underneath)
- Stacking too many mysteries without ever resolving one → reader
  fatigue
- Explaining the mystery in the same chapter it's introduced

---

### Warmth (温情)

**Formula:** `Sustained distance + one silent act of closing it`

**Mechanism:** The reader cries without being asked to. The *smallness*
of the act is what works.

**On-page markers:**
- The act is physical and minor: pouring tea, fixing a collar, picking
  up a fallen thing
- No verbal acknowledgment
- Other characters around them keep doing other things — the moment
  is private

**Pitfalls:**
- Declaring the warmth ("他终于明白了她的好") → ruins it
- Making the gesture too grand → it stops being warmth, becomes melodrama

---

### Awe (敬畏)

**Formula:** `Diminished self + magnified world`

**Mechanism:** The reader feels small *with* the character. Awe is
humility, not fear.

**On-page markers:**
- Long sentence describing the scale, then a single short sentence with
  the protagonist's reaction
- Silence — no dialogue during the encounter
- A detail suggesting time on a scale humans cannot grasp

**Pitfalls:**
- Following up with explanation that shrinks the awesome thing
- Combat against the awe-inducing thing (turns awe into action)

---

### Anger / Injustice (愤怒)

**Formula:** `Unappealable injustice + the victim's silence`

**Mechanism:** The reader's anger is the protagonist's anger. It builds
across chapters.

**On-page markers:**
- The injustice is concrete, specific, not abstract
- The system supporting the injustice is shown to be unmoved
- Onlookers either turn away or quietly approve
- The chapter ends on the perpetrator's casual departure

**Pitfalls:**
- Resolving it in the same chapter kills the cumulative payoff later
- Making the victim too noble — reader becomes condescending, not angry
- Making the perpetrator cartoonish — reader rolls eyes instead of
  seething

---

### Acceptance / Letting Go (释然)

**Formula:** `Long obsession + an unwitnessed farewell`

**Mechanism:** The reader feels relief mixed with sadness. A weight
lifts but leaves a shape.

**On-page markers:**
- Often takes place at night, dawn, or in a familiar empty place
- A physical action accompanies it: burning a letter, leaving an
  object, walking away from a door
- The chapter does not announce that the character has changed — they
  just do something next chapter they couldn't do before

**Pitfalls:**
- Witnessed letting-go is performance, not acceptance
- Explaining what they've let go of (let the reader infer)
- Using this too early (must come after sustained obsession)

## § 2 — Chapter Ending Hooks

The hook is the final 50–200 characters. It does the heavy lifting of
making the reader continue.

### Suspense Hook (悬念)
An unresolved threat or question lands in the final beat.

> 他打开门,看见师父坐在屋里。
> 师父三天前已经死了。
> 师父抬起头,对他笑了笑。

### Reveal Hook (反转)
A piece of information surfaces that recontextualizes the chapter.

> 他走出酒馆,把那枚铜钱抛了出去。
> 铜钱落地,正面朝上。
> 正面刻着的,是十年前那个本应已经死去的人的名字。

### Threat Hook (威胁)
A new danger appears or is named. The danger is concrete, not abstract.

> "你回去的路上,可能会遇到一些人。"老人说,"那些人,不会让你活着回去。"

### Promise Hook (承诺)
A vow or specific future confrontation is named.

> "三年。"她说,"三年后,我会让这座城,听见我的脚步声。"

### Empathy Hook (共情)
A small emotional moment lingers. No twist, no threat — just a feeling
that resonates past the page.

> 风雪夜里,他终于回到了那座小院。
> 屋里灯还亮着。
> 桌上摆着一碗早已凉透的面。

### Hook + Silence (钩子+留白)
A line of dialogue, then nothing. No answer, no narration.

> "你父亲,临死前问过我一句话。"他说,"想不想知道他问的是什么?"

## § 3 — Selection Heuristics (not rules)

These are suggestions, NOT requirements. A planner SHOULD consider them,
but MAY override based on what the chapter actually needs.

| Chapter position | Often suits | Hook often suits |
|------------------|-------------|------------------|
| Arc opening | Mystery, Awe | Threat, Suspense |
| Early arc (setup) | Warmth, mild Sadness | Empathy, Suspense |
| Midpoint | Anger, deeper Mystery | Reveal, Threat |
| Climax buildup | Hype, Mystery | Promise, Hook+Silence |
| Climax | Excitement, Sadness, Hype | Reveal, Empathy |
| Aftermath | Acceptance, lingering Sadness | Empathy, forward Suspense |
| Bridge | Warmth, light Mystery | Empathy, Hook+Silence |

**Rotation guidelines:**
- A chapter SHOULD NOT use the same primary emotion as the previous
  chapter, unless intentionally deepening (two consecutive Mystery
  chapters where the second deepens, not repeats, the first)
- A hook type SHOULD NOT appear in three consecutive chapters

---

## § 4 — Layered Emotion (advanced, optional)

The strongest chapters sometimes carry TWO emotions simultaneously —
one primary, one undercurrent. This is OPTIONAL. Single-emotion chapters
are also good.

Common combinations:
- Hype (primary) + Sadness (undercurrent) → tragic defiance
- Warmth (primary) + Mystery (undercurrent) → unease beneath comfort
- Excitement (primary) + Awe (undercurrent) → victory but the world
  remains larger than us
- Mystery (primary) + Warmth (undercurrent) → we don't trust them, but
  for a moment we do

A writer SHOULD NOT force layering. If the chapter naturally carries
two emotions, layering happens. If not, single-emotion is fine.
